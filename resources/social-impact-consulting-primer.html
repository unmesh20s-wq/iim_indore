Social Impact Consulting Primer

Design recommendations around outcomes, affected communities, and implementation realities.

Source
180DC IIM Indore
Level
Beginner
Format
Practical guide

In this guide

  1. Define the outcome
  2. Build a theory of change
  3. Use evidence responsibly
  4. Design for implementation
  5. Project checklist
01

Define the outcome and stakeholders.

Begin with whose situation should improve, how it should change, and over what period. The organisation's mission and the experience of affected communities should shape the problem definition.

  • Involve affected stakeholders in understanding the problem.
  • Map beneficiaries, delivery partners, funders, and decision-makers.
  • Identify groups that could face unintended costs or exclusion.
02

Build a theory of change.

Connect the resources and work of the organisation to the outcomes it seeks. This chain makes hidden assumptions easier to test.

Inputs → activities → outputs → outcomes → impact

  • Make assumptions and external dependencies explicit.
  • Test whether proposed activities address the identified root causes.
  • Distinguish immediate outputs from changes in behaviour or wellbeing.
03

Use evidence responsibly.

Measure outcomes, not only activity volumes. Combine quantitative indicators with qualitative feedback to understand what changed and for whom.

  • Establish a baseline before assessing progress.
  • Collect only necessary data and protect privacy and consent.
  • Avoid claiming causality when the evidence only shows association.
04

Design for implementation.

A theoretically strong recommendation still fails when it ignores capacity, budget, ownership, or local context.

  • Assign a clear owner, resources, timeline, and feedback channel.
  • Pilot uncertain ideas before committing to scale.
  • Define criteria for adapting, scaling, or stopping the intervention.

Mini project checklist

Before recommending action

  • Is the intended outcome specific?
  • Were affected stakeholders consulted?
  • Are the mechanism and assumptions explicit?
  • Do indicators measure outcomes as well as outputs?
  • Are consent, privacy, equity, and unintended effects addressed?
  • Is there a feasible owner, pilot plan, and review point?
