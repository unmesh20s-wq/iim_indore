Guesstimation 101

Turn an ambiguous question into a transparent, defensible estimate.

Source
180DC IIM Indore
Level
Beginner
Format
Practical guide

In this guide

  1. Define the target
  2. Build the equation
  3. Make useful assumptions
  4. Calculate and communicate
  5. Mini example
01

Define the target.

A strong estimate starts by making the question precise. Clarify what is being counted before choosing a method or a number.

  • Confirm the unit, geography, audience, and time period.
  • Separate total demand from the realistically addressable portion.
  • Decide whether the decision needs a midpoint, a range, or a directional comparison.
02

Build the equation.

Write the structure before adding numbers. Each branch should represent a distinct driver that can be estimated independently.

Population x adoption x frequency x units per use

  • Use a top-down demand-side model when population and behaviour drive the answer.
  • Use a bottom-up supply-side model when capacity, outlets, or throughput are easier to observe.
  • When possible, use the second method as a cross-check rather than averaging two weak estimates.
03

Make useful assumptions.

Assumptions should be understandable and easy to revise. Start from familiar anchors, use rounded numbers, and state why each major input is reasonable.

  • Identify the two or three assumptions that drive most of the result.
  • Create high and low cases for those sensitive inputs.
  • Avoid false precision when the underlying evidence is directional.
04

Calculate and communicate.

Keep units visible through every step. After calculating, run a sanity check against capacity, everyday experience, or another known benchmark.

  • Lead with the estimate or range.
  • Explain the structure and the largest uncertainty.
  • End with what the number implies for the decision.

Mini example

Monthly cups of tea sold on campus

Assume 3,500 students and staff, 26 active days per month, 55% buying tea on a typical day, and 1.2 cups per buyer.

3,500 x 26 x 55% x 1.2 = about 60,000 cups

If daily adoption ranges from 45% to 65%, the estimate becomes roughly 49,000 to 71,000 cups per month. The range is more useful than a falsely precise midpoint.
